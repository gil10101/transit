STANDINGS · CLOSED JUDGED DAYS ONLY

Reliability, city by city

A 0–100 composite over every closed judged day, weighted by the service each route runs. Tokyo leads at 98.8, then Zurich 93.6 and Helsinki 92.2; SF Bay closes the table at 60.5. Each service day is scored against the timetable that was live that day.

On-time = arrival within −1 to +5 min of schedule. Excess wait = time beyond the scheduled headway on frequent service. Added (unscheduled) trips count toward volume, never toward on-time.

PIPELINE · FIVE LAYERS

What happens to one poll

Every number on this page started as a single HTTP request to an agency. Eight poller containers held 30 endpoints at a 30-second floor for a month, and each response was archived verbatim before anything interpreted it — so every layer below can be rebuilt from the one above without asking an agency for the same bytes twice.

FEEDS · WHAT EACH CITY SENDS

Thirty endpoints, eight shapes

No two agencies publish the same thing. Raw volume is each city's seven days of polling to 2026-09-12, while every feed was live, and a feed's size says more about how it is published than about how much service the city runs: Zurich ships a whole national network on every poll, Tokyo ships one JSON array of trains. The vehicle column is what was in motion at once: the most trips in progress in any single poll-minute, by mode, across three weekdays — a trip counts from its first scheduled stop until its last prediction passes, one vehicle each. Where a city also publishes positions, the small print says what those confirm; vehicles reporting with no trip (deadhead, layover, yard) are noted and never folded in.

METHOD · HOW THE SCORING WORKS

Same rules for every city

One ruler, applied the same way in eight time zones. Each feed is matched to the timetable its agency published for that day, and every threshold below is fixed in code, not tuned per city.

−1 / +5min

On time

−5 −1 +5 +15

An arrival counts when it lands between one minute early and five minutes late against the agency's own schedule.

20days

Before a score

No composite until a city has 20 closed, judged service days. All eight cities cleared it.

50%

Completeness floor

A route whose feed carried fewer than half its scheduled trips is set aside, as is any route under 10 scheduled trips a day.

local

Each city on its own clock

Every hour and service day is read in the city's own time zone, so 3am in Tokyo compares to 3am in New York.

What goes into a score

0–100, four parts, fixed weights
35%Excess wait Minutes riders wait beyond the scheduled gap on frequent service; ten minutes scores zero
30%On time Share of stop events inside −1 to +5 minutes
20%Cancellations Share of scheduled trips the agency cancelled
15%Bunching Share of arrivals less than half a scheduled gap behind the one ahead
